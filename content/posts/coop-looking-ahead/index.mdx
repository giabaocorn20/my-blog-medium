This is the last post of the co-op diary. Thank you to everyone who read along! Let's wrap up with what I'd tell the version of me who was staring at that "data role" offer wondering if it was a detour (・ω・)

## What I'd tell my past self

- **It's not a detour.** The 180 I was worried about turned out to be mostly a change of tools. The thinking carried over.
- **Ask the "dumb" question earlier.** Every time I waited, it cost more than asking would have.
- **Verify before you celebrate.** A green checkmark and a correct number are two different things. Check the number.
- **Write things down.** Notes, names for things, where stuff lives. It's boring and it's worth it every time.
- **Get used to being a beginner.** You'll be one again with every new tool. It gets less scary, not less frequent.
- **You don't have to be perfect.** (I say this as a perfectionist, so take it with a grain of salt. I'm working on it.)

## Where this points next

Here's the part that surprised me. I assumed data and hardware were two separate lanes. They're really one loop.

Hardware produces data: battery logs, test flights, sensor readings. Somebody has to turn that into something a person can make a decision from. Back when I was working on drone projects and battery management, a lot of the hard questions were really data questions. How is the battery behaving? Did that change help? Was that flight actually better than the last one?

Now I have a much better idea of how to answer them. Much data, very hardware, wow (see top of page, the doge understands).

So the plan for me is to keep one foot in each: build the hardware, and be the person who can read what it's telling me. I think that combination is where I'd like to end up. I'll write about it as it happens.

Thanks again for reading. If you're in the middle of your own 180, you'll be fine. Happy building! ٩(◕‿◕)۶
